Getting started

How to read tarot without pretending

Tarot is a structured way of looking at a situation you are already in. The cards do not know anything you do not — what they do is interrupt the story you have been telling yourself long enough for a different reading to become possible. That is genuinely useful, and it does not require believing anything in particular.

Ask a question that can be answered

The single biggest determinant of whether a reading is useful is the question. "What does my future hold" produces fog. "What am I not seeing about this job offer" produces something you can act on.

  • Open beats closed. "What should I know about…" gives the cards room. "Will he call" does not.
  • Ask about you. The cards read your situation, not another person's private mind.
  • One question at a time. Two questions in one spread produce an answer to neither.
  • Avoid asking twice. Re-drawing until you like the answer is how you learn nothing.

Position first, card second

The same card means different things in different seats. The Tower in outcome is a warning; The Tower in what is passing out of influence is relief. Read the position's question first, then ask what this card says in answer to it. Beginners reverse that order and end up with a pile of definitions instead of a reading.

Reversals are not opposites

A reversed card is usually the same force turned inward, blocked, or badly timed — not its negation. Reversed Strength is not weakness; it is strength applied against yourself. If reversals feel like noise, turn them off. A 78-card deck read upright only is a complete system.

Read the spread as a whole

This is the step most people skip, and it is where the answer usually is. Before interpreting any single card, look at the shape:

  • Major arcana count. Many majors means the situation is structural and largely outside your daily control. Few or none means it is practical, and you have levers.
  • Suit balance. A spread heavy in Pentacles is about material reality; heavy in Swords, about what you are thinking. Solutions from the wrong register slide off.
  • Reversal density. Mostly reversed usually means blocked energy rather than absent energy.
  • Repeated numbers. Three fives is a spread about loss and adjustment, whatever the suits say.

When a reading is bad news

Death, The Tower and the Ten of Swords are the cards people dread, and they are usually the most useful ones in the deck. Each of them describes something that is already over being recognised as over. That is uncomfortable and it is not a prediction — it is a description of where you already are.

Try it with a real question. Three cards is enough structure to see a trajectory without drowning in detail.
